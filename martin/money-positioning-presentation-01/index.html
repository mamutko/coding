Flexible Money Positioning

A layer of indirection for moving money between banks — safely, transparently, and by configuration rather than by emergency.

The Problem

A fixed plan of money movements

We processed payroll for our customers. Each submission generated a plan of money movements — transactions from account A to account B. It worked well, but lacked flexibility.

March 2023

Silicon Valley Bank collapses

By Thursday, March 9 it was unclear whether SVB would process any further transactions. Treasury directed us to move all funds out — immediately.

The Weakness Exposed

It worked — but it was fragile

We mitigated our exposure, but moving money quickly required heavy engineering. The rescue scripts:

  • Lived outside the product
  • Were not covered by automated testing
  • Were easy to use wrongly
  • Lacked proper auditability

We needed a simple, configurable system to move funds between banks safely and transparently.

Collecting Requirements

Many uses, one solution

Looking past the immediate crisis, we gathered requirements — and several distinct use cases kept pointing at the same thing: a flexible system for positioning money across banks.

  • Crisis Management — reposition money quickly without engineering involvement
  • Risk Grouping — group customers for independent risk management e.g. “group A” vs “group B”
  • Idle Funds — sweep idle money into investment accounts
  • Operational Constraints — handle the quirks of specific bank accounts → this one became the driver for taking the idea to production

The Key Idea

Every dollar has a purpose

Decouple the money we hold from the accounts holding it. Classifiers assign a purpose; a separate configuration maps each purpose to a physical account. Change circumstances? Just update the configuration — the system moves the money.

An Interlude

A pause — then a new partner

With SVB out we had a single banking partner, and money positioning is moot with one bank. JPMorgan Chase was chosen as the new partner; integration began (a separate story).

Summer 2025

The Hackathon — Project BEACON

A cross-functional team — engineering, product, and critically Jason, Director of Finance & Risk — so any prototype met treasury requirements and had a path to production.

My proof-of-concept

Classifier rules · a configuration mapping purposes to accounts · a scheduled task moving funds while maintaining consistency. Demo classifiers: wage, tax, cheque, investment. Built & demoed in days.

September 2025

From hackathon to production priority

Wages via JPM already worked, but some tax payment types did not:

The gaps

Cheques & CTX — required for NM SIT (New Mexico state income tax).

NY MCTMT — one cheque for all client liabilities → all funds must reside in the same bank.

Full feature parity on JPM would be expensive. Money positioning solved both, elegantly.

Taking it to Production

One engineer hardened the POC, another updated the UI. Three sprints, three classifier rules: NM SIT, cheque payments, NY MCTMT.

Design Decisions

  • Dark Launch — shipped dormant first / enable / gradual rollout
  • Idempotent — operations can be safely re-run without moving money twice
  • Request Cache — optimizations to make it run in ~20min on the whole corpus

Tradeoffs

  • No Classifier UI — consciously avoided; classifiers implemented in Python
  • Start Narrow — begin with classifiers for the immediate needs NM SIT, cheque payments, NY MCTMT

Where it stands

Clean. Predictable. Testable.

Entirely backend. UI-based classifier config was intentionally kept out of scope to preserve simplicity and testability. Three rules today — ample room to expand.

Introducing the simple concept of “purpose” let us decompose a complex operational challenge into a set of clear, elegant rules.
