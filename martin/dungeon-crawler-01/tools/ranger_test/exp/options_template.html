What the tests say

8 × 8 in one pass works. 64 of 64 cells match the guide's stride, every row faces the right way, and it is one consistent character in all directions for $0.055, about a third of five single-direction images. The figures are about 200 px tall instead of about 390 px; the game draws them at about 118 px. In S and N, 4 of 16 cells have both feet at nearly the same height where the guide has one clearly lower (the known front and back weakness).

10 and 12 frames match every frame at the same price per image. The game needs no code change: frames come from meta.json, and fpt scales with the frame count. The hero walks 4.2 tiles/s and already plays 8 frames at about 21 fps; villagers walk 1.6 tiles/s and play 8 frames at about 8 fps, where 12 frames look visibly smoother.

The faded foot is opaque. OpenAI sometimes paints the far boot pale, desaturated and without an outline (alpha about 250, so not see-through). "Every part fully OPAQUE: no faded, ghostly or see-through limbs" in the prompt removed it in all four new images. grid_check.py flags the clear case; milder ones still need a look on the dark floor.

Guides: render_guide.py (Quaternius Universal Animation Library, Walk_Loop, CC0) and walk_cycle.py (stick figure). Prompts: guide_prompt.py. Numbers: grid_check.py (foot spread = width of the bottom 12% of the figure / its height). Frames are cut by the guide's grid cells; the guide puts each body at its cell's centre, so the players show the painted motion without re-anchoring.
